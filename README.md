### **ABOUT ME**

</div>

I'm **Arda**, known online as **Wiezty**.

I'm interested in understanding how things work — from software and artificial intelligence to science, philosophy, and the ideas that shape the way we understand the world.

I don't like limiting curiosity to a single field.

Programming is one of the ways I create and experiment, but it is only one part of what interests me.

I'm drawn to questions that don't always have simple answers:

> How does intelligence emerge?
> Where are the limits of technology?
> Can something truly be impossible?
> How much of what we know is actually understood?
> What happens when different fields of knowledge intersect?

For me, learning isn't about collecting titles or knowing everything.

It's about **being curious enough to keep asking questions.**

### **HOW I THINK**

</div>

I don't believe knowledge belongs to isolated categories.

**Science** can lead to philosophy.
**Philosophy** can lead to better questions.
**Better questions** can lead to better science.
**Technology** can turn ideas into something real.

That's the part that interests me most:

### *the space between disciplines.*

<div align="center">

### **CREATING**

</div>

I enjoy taking an idea that doesn't exist yet and trying to turn it into something tangible.

Sometimes that means writing software.

Sometimes it means researching something I don't understand.

Sometimes it means questioning an assumption everyone else accepts.

And sometimes it simply means following an idea to see where it leads.

I don't need an idea to be practical before exploring it.

<br>

> ## *“Impossible is often just a word we use for things we haven't figured out yet.”*

</div>

I don't believe everything is possible.

But I believe the boundary between **possible** and **impossible** is worth challenging.

Every invention once existed only as an idea.

Every scientific discovery began with a question.

Every piece of technology was once something that didn't exist.

So I prefer to ask:

<div align="center">

### **“Why not?”**

</div>

before deciding:

<div align="center">
